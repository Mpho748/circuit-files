Case 02 • Sabotage

Can your circuit survive?

A circuit that works perfectly tells you less than a circuit you deliberately break.

You have one supply and three identical lamps. A saboteur will remove exactly ONE lamp after your circuit is complete.

Create the arrangement that suffers the least possible disruption. Test at least three genuinely different arrangements.

🔒 Game Master checkpoint

“The other lamps stayed on” is only an observation. Explain what about the wiring made that possible.

Case 03 • Pattern Hunt

Find something nobody told you to measure.

Choose one arrangement. Create versions with 1, 2 and 3 identical lamps.

Your team decides what is worth observing or measuring. Find a pattern strong enough to make a prediction.

NEW EVIDENCE REQUIRED

Do not test four lamps yet. Rearrange the wiring into the OTHER circuit type from Case 01.

🔒 Game Master checkpoint

Present a pattern, a prediction and a test that could have proved your original idea wrong.

Case 04 • Current Crime Scene

Three readings. No labels.

A technician took three current readings from one circuit containing two identical lamps:

0.90 A   •   0.45 A   •   0.45 A

The measuring-position labels were destroyed. Construct a circuit in which all three readings can be true. You decide where to measure.

🔒 Game Master checkpoint

The Game Master may choose a new measuring position. Your explanation must predict what happens there.

Case 05 • The 9 V Dispute

Two scientists. Two confident claims.

The supply is set to 9 V.

Scientist A: “Every lamp always has 9 V across it because the supply is 9 V.”

Scientist B: “The 9 V is always divided between the lamps.”

Your case file says neither statement works for every circuit.

Build whatever circuits you need. Use a voltmeter to determine when each claim appears true and when it fails.

🔒 Game Master checkpoint

Your evidence must contain actual voltage measurements.

Final Case • The Black Box

The wiring is hidden.

You cannot see the circuit. You only know how it behaves. Reconstruct it.

Recovered evidence

① Three identical lamps use one supply.

② Removing Lamp 1 does not stop Lamps 2 or 3.

③ Current leaving the supply is greater than the current through one lamp.

④ The potential difference across each lamp equals the supply potential difference.

⑤ There are places where current separates and later comes together again.

Reconstruct the invisible circuit

Build your proposed circuit in PhET. Then deliberately try to prove your own model wrong.

🔓 Scientific terminology unlocked

The two arrangements you have investigated are called SERIES CIRCUITS and PARALLEL CIRCUITS.

Do not copy definitions. Construct the rules from your evidence.

🏠 BOSS LEVEL — The House

A newly wired house has a serious problem. When the kitchen lamp fails, the bedroom lights, television and refrigerator also stop working.

Diagnose the wiring, redesign it in PhET, deliberately cause one component to fail, and demonstrate that your solution works.

CASE CLOSED only when every team member can defend the final design using experimental evidence.
